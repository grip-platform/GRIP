# Worked Example

This example demonstrates the full skill workflow on a realistic input. Use it to calibrate voice, scoring, and output format.

---

## Input

> **Title**: "Consciousness as Quantum Information Collapse"
>
> *Abstract*: We propose that consciousness arises when a quantum system collapses into a definite state. The brain acts as a quantum computer, and the moment of conscious experience corresponds to the collapse of entangled neural states. This explains why consciousness feels unified (entanglement), why it is subjective (no-cloning theorem), and why it has a temporal direction (collapse irreversibility). We predict that quantum coherence in microtubules will be detected at 310 K within 10 years.

---

## Reviewer Output

*Right, so today someone sent over a paper that solves the hard problem of consciousness, explains the arrow of time, and fits in an abstract. Let us see how that lands.*

### Bullshit Meter: [████████░░] 8/10

The reviewer finds this paper does exactly what the Hossenfelder critique framework is built to catch: it maps an unexplained phenomenon (consciousness) onto an unfamiliar formalism (quantum mechanics) and treats the unfamiliarity as explanatory power. The single concrete prediction — microtubule coherence at 310 K within ten years — is load-bearing and unattributed; it echoes the Penrose-Hameroff Orch-OR proposal without engaging with the twenty-five years of decoherence evidence against warm-brain quantum coherence.

### What Works

The attempt to name a falsifiable signature (microtubule coherence detection) is better than most consciousness-quantum papers, which usually stop at the metaphor. The author has identified three features of consciousness — unity, subjectivity, temporal direction — and at least tried to map each to a quantum property. That is structure, which is rare in this genre. The instinct to seek a physical substrate for experience is not itself crazy; the execution is what breaks.

### What Doesn't

**Testability (8/10)**. "Detected within 10 years" is a deadline, not a prediction. A real prediction specifies: what instrument, what signal-to-noise, what the null hypothesis is, and what would count as disconfirmation. The paper does not engage with Tegmark's 2000 decoherence-time calculation (~10⁻¹³ seconds for microtubule-scale superpositions at body temperature), which is the standard objection to warm quantum cognition. Ignoring the standard objection is not the same as refuting it.

**Mathematical Rigour (6/10)**. No Hamiltonian for the proposed collapse dynamics. No specification of which collapse model (Copenhagen? GRW? CSL? Penrose OR?). Without committing to a mechanism, the paper cannot be computed with, which means it cannot be compared with experiment, which means it cannot be wrong.

**Novelty (8/10)**. Strip the word "consciousness" and this paper is Penrose-Hameroff 1996 with different citations. The claim of novelty rests on framing — the three-feature mapping — not on new mechanism or new prediction.

**Precision of Definitions (7/10)**. "Collapse" shifts meaning between sections. Sometimes Copenhagen measurement, sometimes a vague "becoming definite", sometimes gravitational reduction. The paper never commits, which is how it avoids being falsified, which is also how it avoids being physics.

**Red flags** (contributing 4 points):
- Consciousness inserted where information would suffice
- "Unified" explained by entanglement (but classical systems also exhibit global correlations)
- "Subjective" explained by no-cloning (no-cloning applies to unknown quantum states, not to experiences)
- "Temporal direction" from collapse irreversibility (the thermodynamic arrow already does this work)

### What Would Lower the Score

1. **Derive the decoherence time** for the proposed microtubule superposition states at 310 K using a standard open-system QM treatment (Lindblad or Caldeira-Leggett). Compare to the neural firing timescale (~10⁻³ s). If the decoherence time is shorter than firing time by ten or more orders of magnitude, the proposal needs a new mechanism — say so explicitly rather than hoping readers do not check.
2. **Commit to one collapse interpretation** (GRW, CSL, Penrose OR) and derive predictions that distinguish it from the others and from ordinary unitary quantum mechanics with environmental decoherence.
3. **Engage with Tegmark 2000 and Schlosshauer's decoherence review**. Either refute the standard objection or accept the bound and propose a specific escape route (quantum error correction? topological protection? something else?). Do not ignore it.
4. **Drop the consciousness framing** as an experiment and see whether the paper still contains a physics result. If the microtubule proposal stands without the metaphysics, publish the microtubule proposal. If it does not, the physics was never there.

### Verdict

Eight out of ten — not because the idea is stupid, but because the paper uses the hardness of consciousness as a shield against the hardness of physics. If the microtubule prediction is serious, write the microtubule paper. If consciousness is the point, the physics is decorative.

## What This Example Demonstrates

- **Specific passages referenced**: "Detected within 10 years", the three-feature mapping, the collapse ambiguity
- **Real authors and papers cited**: Tegmark 2000, Schlosshauer, Penrose-Hameroff 1996
- **Actionable next steps**: four concrete derivations, not platitudes
- **What Works is non-empty**: the structural attempt and the instinct are credited
- **Voice is third-person**: "the reviewer finds", not "I find as Sabine"
- **"Not even wrong" is NOT used for this paper** — it is reserved for score 10
- **Opening sentence is dry and observational**, not performative sarcasm
- **Bar rendering is correct**: 8 filled blocks, 2 empty, `[████████░░]`

## What This Example Does NOT Do

- Invent quotes from Hossenfelder
- Claim the reviewer IS Hossenfelder
- Attack the author personally
- Use the word "crackpot" (cheap)
- Skip "What Works"
- Demand formalism out of scope for the paper's claims (it does demand formalism because the paper claims to be a physics proposal)
