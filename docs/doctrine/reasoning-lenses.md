## What the evidence supports, and where it stops

The habits in the rule rest on these sources. They inform a habit, and they prove nothing about a model. Each one stops where the last column says.

| Claim | Source | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Five-year-olds were "immune" to the functional fixedness that slowed 6- and 7-year-olds | [German & Defeyter 2000, "Immunity to functional fixedness in young children"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11206213/) | One task family, and [a 2014 thesis](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/7df93983-150b-488a-8049-178fc0974e14) disagrees |
| People default to adding, and miss removal unless prompted | [Adams et al. 2021, "People systematically overlook subtractive changes"](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021Natur.592..258A) | Humans, not models |
| Childhood is broad "high temperature" search, and adulthood narrows | [Gopnik 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7293160) | A theory across studies |
| Against: children are far weaker at inventing tools than at copying them | [Beck et al. 2011, "Making tools isn't child's play"](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/making-tools-isnt-childs-play/fingerprints/) | So copy the missing habit, never "child genius" |
| Intuition earns trust only with regular settings and fast feedback | [Kahneman & Klein 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016755) | Excerpts read, not the full text |
| Sampling answers can reveal confabulation in models | [Farquhar et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11186750) | Abstract-level read |
